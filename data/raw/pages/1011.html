    Sustainable Initiatives

    To help you live more sustainably, we have implemented a variety of programs, initiatives, and features within our campuses that promote environmental, economic, and social sustainability. These programs are designed to provide affordable, green, and easy to use options for students.

    Working with over 30 internal departments, student groups, and community partners, we work diligently to ensure that our programs are accessible, support the strategic goals of the university and help create a positive campus experience for you!

    Waste Reduction and Circularity

    One of the best ways to reduce our impact on the environment is to limit the items going into landfill. By simply adhering to the 5 R’s of sustainability (refuse, reduce, reuse, rot, and recycle) we can create tangible change on campus.

    Each of us produces garbage every day and leading a zero-waste lifestyle can be daunting, but it doesn’t have to be. We’re here to show you the options available on campus, including how to use them, where to find them and why its important to participate.

    To achieve a zero-waste Laurier, it needs to be a community effort and as the largest population on campus, students can make the most significant impact: 

    • Freestores in Waterloo and Brantford
    • Waste Sorting and Recycling
    • Move Out program

    Sustainable Grounds

    With our Waterloo and Brantford campuses both being located within city centres, Laurier is challenged with maximizing the naturalization of our outdoor spaces. This means including more plants indigenous to the respective regions and supporting biodiversity, primarily pollinator species. Pollinators play an important role in supporting local food systems.

    The benefits of having more naturalized spaces on campus address multiple sustainability issues ranging from increasing biodiversity, supporting stormwater management and preventing flooding, reduce noise pollution, absorbing carbon from the air, reducing the heat island effect, and support mental well-being.

    Did You Know that Laurier is Pollinator-Friendly?

    Laurier’s Waterloo campus is a designated Bee Campus which recognizes our commitment to supporting pollinator habitats. The Northdale Campus features a pollinator garden and pollinator spiral with more than 20 native plant and flower species specifically designed to mimic native habitats. These spaces provide shelter, food, and a place for pollinators to rest while traveling in larger urban spaces. These gardens and the adjacent community garden are also surrounded by naturalized spaces that reflect the land pre-development. We encourage students to come out and enjoy all Northdale has to offer!

    Outside Northdale, native plants and pollinator species can be found braided into the campus aesthetic including outside Lazaridis Hall, MacDonald House, Wilson Hall, the Science building, and many other spaces.

    On the Brantford campus, Laurier has an apiary with four bee hives installed on the roof of One Market housing more than 320,000 bees. As we work towards having the Brantford campus designated as a Bee Campus, we are working to implement more naturalization and pollinator friendly projects on the university grounds.

    Interested in volunteering with the Laurier Apiary? Reach out by email to sustainability@wlu.ca or through Instagram @laurier_apiary. 

    Sustainable Transportation

    Laurier offers a variety of programs and initiatives to support a safe and environmentally friendly commute to campus. Alternative modes of transportation are not only good for the environment but also helps to clear up traffic congestion allowing for a quicker commute, and you don’t have to worry about finding parking on campus.

    • Public transportation: student transit passes
    • Alternative transportation: secure bike storage
    • Vehicle use: campus EV stations, car share program, and student carpool program

    Sustainable Food

    Sustainable food systems help support a healthy lifestyle on campus. Some of our initiatives include the Northdale community garden, Fair Trade food options, and the Cool Foods program.

    Sustainable Health

    Health and well-being is an intersection of sustainability. All of our sustainability initiatives feed into the promotion of your health and well-being. 

    The Menstrual Equity Program aims to ensure that all Laurier students with capacity for menstruation have access to free period products on our campus.
