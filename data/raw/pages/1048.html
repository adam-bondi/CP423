    Laurier Off-Campus University Students

    Whether you commute from out of town, live with family, or moved to off-campus student housing, the Laurier Off-Campus University Students (LOCUS) program is here to connect you to the campus community. 

    The LOCUS program at our Brantford, Milton and Waterloo campuses work specifically to support our first-year off-campus students (including mature and transfer students) with events, upper-year student mentors and provide opportunities to get involved.

     

    Get Involved with LOCUS

    LOCUS is a free program for all first-year off-campus students that provides academic, social, and personal support to ensure you don't miss Laurier’s on-campus experience. Just like a residence floor has a residence don to guide and mentor their first-year students, off-campus first-year students have off-campus advisors (OCAs).

    There are so many ways to get involved on campus through LOCUS:

    • Attend our programming and events throughout the year to make connections and get comfortable in our campus community.
    • Find your community by opting in to a faculty or interest-based LOCUS Link
    • Join one of the student councils to gain leadership skills and meet other students.
    • Connect with one of our off-campus advisors to learn how to make the most of your student experience.
    • Find us on Instagram @locuslaurier and give us a follow to easily keep up to date with all our upcoming events!

    All LOCUS students will also receive reminders to their @mylaurier.ca email account.

    Looking to get involved as an upper-year student? Consider our work and volunteer positions to help support our incoming first-year students.

    A Part of the Lynx Pack

    Each residence building and LOCUS are assigned a mascot as part of the FYLP. The mascot for the LOCUS program is a lynx. When you join LOCUS, we like to say that you have joined the Lynx Pack.

    Programming and Events

    LOCUS provides a fantastic opportunity for you to meet other off-campus students, learn about Laurier through engaging events, and have a mentor for you to ask about all things Laurier!

    Starting in September, our off-campus advisors (OCAs) will facilitate a variety of events throughout the year which will be focused on building connections, meeting new people, academic success, student wellness, and/or getting to know the campus and surrounding community.

    We're always looking for event ideas. If there is something that you really want to do during your first year at Laurier, let an OCA know and we'll try to make it happen.

    Social: Our social events are focused on helping you build connections, meet other first-year students and create lasting friendships.

    Some events that we host include game and trivia nights, open gym, karaoke, SkyZone, and fantasy sports.

    Community and Campus Integration: Discover the city you're living in and connect with various campus groups through events like campus trivia or bingo nights, skating in the community or eating at a local eatery.

    Personal Well-Being: Destress and prioritize your well-being with events like yoga and meditation, or cooking classes. The Student Experience team also hosts events throughout the year to help you maintain your wellness.

    Academic: Connect with academic resources and improve your study habits. Study hours and time management sessions will help you stay on top of your studies, while programs like business pitch competitions and Job Hunting 101 enhance your academic experience and help prepare you for life after Laurier.

    Join a LOCUS Link!

    A LOCUS Link is a themed LOCUS community where off-campus first-year students who share a common interest, major, or coursework can come together. First-year undergraduate students who are living off-campus (LOCUS students) can express their interest in opting in to a LOCUS Link, and one of our team members will reach out to confirm if their placement is accepted.

    Each Link is led by an upper-year student (Off-Campus Advisor) who is in the same academic field or shares the same hobby/interest as the group. Links are currently available on our Brantford and Waterloo campuses.

    By joining a LOCUS Link, you can expect to receive numerous programming/events tailored to the Link's theme, the opportunity to connect more frequently and casually with your Link Community, an assigned Off-Campus Advisor (OCA) for more direct mentorship throughout your first year, and more.

    The expression of interest form is now open and will close on September 21st at 11:59 p.m. Students will be placed in Links on a first-come, first-served basis. Limited space is available in each Link. Students can rank more than one choice (if they wish) for the Links available on their campus. 
    Filling out the form earlier is beneficial to increase the chance of there being space available in your first-choice Link. Additionally, by expressing interest sooner, students will be able to receive direct promotion and attend Link events beginning during Laurier Orientation

    View the available links below. Click here to access the opt-in form for the 2026-2027 academic year!

    Are you enrolled in the Bachelor of Social Work program on Laurier's Brantford campus?  

    Are you passionate about human rights and social justice that empower members of society?  

    The Social Work (BSW) Link is a program-specific experience offered to Laurier-Brantford students enrolled in their first year of the BSW program and living off-campus. With the guidance of an Off-Campus Advisor, an upper-year BSW student, first-year students will work together throughout the year to build community, focus on the balance of academic and social programming, and connect students to campus and community resources.  

    The Link’s facilitated programming and learning opportunities are designed to engage likeminded individuals, and develop first-year students by providing tools for success in the program and focus on practice-based learning, which includes the principles of human rights and social justice to empower members of society, particularly those who are disadvantaged by the social dynamics of class, race, gender, age, ability etc. 

    Highlights: 

    • Connections with faculty and alumni 
    • Community involvement and related programming 
    • Placement questions and campus referrals  
    • Specific programming based on academic interest 
    • Community among social work students 
    • Fostering a deeper connection with peers and OCA, promoting asking questions and providing referrals  

    Whether you're a casual or serial gamer, or a retired one looking to recreate the joys of your childhood, join the LOCUS Gaming Link! We'll be playing games and getting to know other Laurier students all year long; you don't want to miss out!   

    The Gaming Link is an interest-based experience offered to students in the first year of their undergraduate degree and living off-campus. This Link will be led by an Off-Campus Advisor, an upper-year student with similar interests in online gaming. Students in this Link will be able to meet other first-year students with similar interests and build community through social programming. The Off-Campus Advisor leading the Link is also there to help students focus on finding their work/life balance at university and to provide guidance to the students throughout their first year at Laurier. 

    Highlights: 

    • Community involvement and related programming
    • Fostering a deeper connection with peers and OCA, promoting asking questions and providing referrals  
    • Specific programming based on interest (gaming – e.g. tournaments, regular game play) 
    • Community among off-campus students 
    • Opportunity to explore cross-campus connections through Gaming Link and online programming 

    Do you love trying a new craft? What about discussing films or novels? Whether you are interested in the world of creative arts or would love to cozy up with a book, this might be the Link for you!  

    The Arts and Literature Link is an interest-based community focused on the various types of arts, such as visual, music, drama, photography, and more. In addition, this group can focus on various types of literature, hosting book clubs, book swaps/blind dates with a book, attending related community events, and more. While this Link seems broad, that is because it will be tailored to the students' wants. A survey that students complete with registration will be available to help the OCA see what types of arts and literature are of the most interest to this cohort. 

    Highlights: 

    • Community involvement and related programming
    • Fostering a deeper connection with peers and OCA, promoting asking questions and providing referrals  
    • Specific programming based on interest (various arts and literature – e.g. events focused on painting/visual art, photography, music, reading) 
    • Community among off-campus students
