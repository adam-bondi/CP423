    Step 1: Registration and Documentation

    Step 1 in registering with Accessible Learning requires you to do the following:

    1. Review Disability Documentation Requirements.
    2. If applicable, review Interim Accommodations, UW Double Degree, or Service Animals.
    3. Complete the Accessible Learning Registration Form.
    4. Submit your documentation.
    5. Promptly respond to messages from Accessible Learning in your myLaurier inbox about next steps.

    Documentation Requirements

    Contact our Intake Team with questions about the documentation you need, the disability category that best reflects your disability, or need our forms in alternate format.

    Students seeking academic accommodations at Laurier due to a disability are required to submit up-to-date documentation to support their request. This documentation helps Accessible Learning:

    • Confirm that you are a person with a disability.
    • Understand how your disability effects your learning at university.

    Individual Education Plans

    An Individual Education Plan (IEP) on its own is not sufficient documentation to access academic accommodations at Laurier.

    Please note:

    • a history of receiving accommodations or having an IEP in high school does not guarantee eligibility for accommodations at university.
    • In accordance with the Ontario Human Rights Code, students must provide current documentation confirming a diagnosed disability to be considered for academic accommodations.

    Disability Categories

    To access the correct form below, select the disability category—or categories—that best reflects your disability. The form must be completewd by a qualified healthcare practitioner who is authorized to diagnose and treat your condition. 

    Important Information

    • You are not required to disclose your medical diagnosis to Accessible Learning when requesting accommodations.
    • Identifying a disability category is not the same as sharing a diagnosis.
    • Disability category information helps Accessible Learning assess your needs and develop appropriate accommodation and support plans.

    Recommended Accommodations

    Healthcare practitioners may recommend academic accommodations based on assessment results and disability-related functional limitations.

    Important: Laurier determines and approves accommodations based on its assessment of a student's disability-related needs within the academic environment.

    Examples of Attention Disabilities

    Common attention-related disabilities include:

    • Attention Deficit Hyperactivity Disorder (ADHD)
    • Attention Deficit Disorder (ADD)

    Documentation Requirements

    Students requesting accommodations for ADHD/ADD at Laurier must provide acceptable documentation which include either:

    • Accessible Learning Disability Verification Form: Attention/ASD Disability(PDF Fillable, completed withinin the last 12 months)
    • Psycho-educational assessment
      (completed using adult measures)

    Diagnostic Criteria

    Documentation must confirm an ADHD/ADD diagnosis based on the following: 

    • Meets the DSM-5 definition
      A persistent pattern of inattention and/or hyperactivity-impulsivity that interferes with functioning or development. 
    • Confirms five (5) or more symptoms of inattention or hyperactivity-impulsivity.  
    • Symptoms: 
      • Were present before 12 years of age1 and persisted into adulthood
      • Reduces functioning in two or more life areas (e.g., home, school, work, social) 
      • Cannot be explained by another mental disorder (e.g., mood, anxiety, dissociative, or personality disorders).

    Assessment Standards 

    Qualified clinicians must base their diagnosis on:  

    • Structured diagnostic interview covering: 
      • Family history.
      • Developmental and academic markers.
      • Co-existing disorders (e.g., mood or anxiety disorders, substance use, neuromedical conditions, symptom exaggeration).
      • Conditions or behaviors that mimic ADHD.
    • Data from multiple sources: student, significant other, parents, school/employment records. 
    • Confirmation that:
      • Symptoms were present prior to age 12 (even if not limiting until adolescence)
      • Symptoms have persisted for the last 12 months.
    • Clinical observations of hyperactivity, impulsivity, and/or distractibility.
    • Evidence of impairment in one or more life areas (social, postsecondary, employment) 
    • Accounting for periods when student was symptom free (if applicable).
    • Details of ADHD medication use and treatment response (if applicable).

    Practitioners must verify either in an assessment report or on the Accessible Learning Disability Verification Form that the student meets these criteria and outline current functional limitations in a postsecondary setting.

    Important Notes

    • Laurier does not accept documentation based solely on symptom checklists or rating scales.
    • Laurier does not accept assessments from online clinics such as Frida and ACE Clinics, as they currently do not meet our standards.      

    Qualified Professionals 

    Documentation must be completed by a healthcare professional trained in differential diagnosis of ADHD and related psychiatric disorders, such as: 

    • Registered psychologist
    • Psychiatrist
    • Family physician
    • Nurse Practitioner

    Mitchell, J. T., et al. (2019). A qualitative analysis of contextual factors relevant to suspected late-onset ADHD. Journal of Attention Disorders, 25(5), 724–735. https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/1087054719837743

    Examples of brain injury include acquired brain injury, concussion, or post-concussive syndrome.

    You may be required to provide updated documentation throughout your degree in order to determine if your disability remains, if functional limitations have changed, and if accommodations are still required.

    Diagnosing a Learning Disability

    A learning disability can only be diagnosed by a qualified psychologist through a psychoeducational assessment.

    For general information about learning disabilities, visit the Learning Disabilities Association of Ontario.

    Required Documentation

    Follow this link to download a copy of these requirements: Laurier Learning Disability Documentation Requirements (PDF)

    Students seeking academic accommodations for a learning disability must submit a psychoeducational assessment completed by a Registered Psychologist or Psychological Associate. The assessment must have been completed using adult measures:

    1. Within the past five (5) years, or
    2. After 18 years of age

    All psychoeducational assessments, regardless of completion date, must include:

    • Appropriate standardized tests of Ability using adult measures (e.g., WAIS-IV).
    • Appropriate standardized tests of Information Processing (e.g., WMS-IV).
    • Appropriate standardized tests of Achievement sampling each of the following achievement areas:
      • Reading (decoding, comprehension, fluency).
      • Writing (mechanics, expression, fluency).
      • Math (calculation, conceptualization, fluency).
      • Memory (working, auditory, visual - immediate and delayed).
    • Evidence of a significant discrepancy between (at least) normal ability and one or more achievement areas (reading, writing or math).
    • Other potential causes for the discrepancy ruled out.
    • Relevant medical, developmental, education, and family history.
    • Behavioural observations. 
    • A statement confirming the validity of testing results.
    • Percentile scores for all standardized measures administered.
    • Test results reported and interpreted. 
    • A clear diagnostic statement.
    • Signatures of the Registered Psychologist or Psychological Associate.

    Note: Assessments missing any of the above elements may not be accepted, possibly delaying a student's registration while Accessible Learning follows up with the issuing assessors.

    Memory Aids

    Students requesting memory aids during exams as an accommodation must ensure their assessments include:

    • Validated measures of performance validity.
    • Memory tests assessing later recall of learned information.
    • Measures evaluating whether cues aided recall.

    For detailed guidance, students and assessors should review our Memory Aid guidelines.

    Other Documentation Requirements

    See below for documentation requirements and information pertaining to service animals, interim accommodations, and double degree programs with the University of Waterloo.

    Students who do not have disability documentation may be eligible for interim accommodations while they work on obtaining the required documentation. Interim accommodations typically expire after one or two terms.

    Documentation

    Students seeking interim accommodations should submit any documentation they have, such as:

    • Grade 12 Individual Education Plans (IEP)
    • Outdated psycho-educational assessments
    • Letters from healthcare practitioners (HCP)

    Healthcare practitioner letters must:

    • Be dated
    • Be printed on original letterhead
    • Include the HCP's signature and license number

    Interm Accommodation Expiry Dates

    • Fall term expire on April 30
    • Winter term expire on August 31
    • Spring/Summer term expire on December 31

    Students must submit updated documentation before their interim accommodations expire to maintain continued support. 

    Students enrolled in a Laurier-University of Waterloo (UW) double degree program who wish to use their academic accommodations for exams must register and book exam accommodations by all published deadlines. 

    Laurier Students Whose Home Institution is UW

    Laurier students with disabilities whose home institution is UW must:

    1. Register with UWAccessAbility Services first.
    2. Register with Accessible Learning once UW has approved their accommodation plan.
    3. Upload UW Verification of Academic Accommodations as documentation to Accessible Learning.

    Accommodation Coordination Across Laurier & UW

    Accessible Learning (Laurier) and AccessAbility Services (UW) will make every effort to ensure accommodations are as consistent as possible across both institutions.

    Once Laurier receives the student's UW accommodation plan:

    • Accessible Learning will enter all easily transferable accommodations into our online system.
    • Students will receive an email at their myLaurier address with instructions on how to access their Laurier accommodations and how to book accommodated exams.

    When Additional Steps Are Required

    Students whose accommodations are more complex or not easily transferable to Laurier's learning enviornment will be required to meet with:

    • An Intake Coordinator
    • An Accommodation Consultant

    Students may also be asked to submit additional documentation, which may include:

    • The documentation already provided to UW, or
    • New or updated documentation

    Please contact the Intake Team with any questions.

    Submit Documentation

    Do not email a copy of your documentation to Accessible Learning. Accessible Learning will not accept emailed or faxed copies of documentation due to security and privacy concerns.

    Prepare Your Documentation

    Documentation must:

    • Include all the pages.
    • Be legible.
    • Be uploaded in a single PDF file.

    Convert photos to a single PDF file if submitting photos of your documentation. Accessible Learning will not review illegible or incomplete documentation. Once your documentation is prepared, follow the instructions below for submitting it to Accessible Learning.

    Include your documentation with your registration form.

    1. Log into Accessible Learning Online
    2. In the Student Application box, click View Application.
    3. From the top navigation bar, click Files.
    4. Follow the instructions to upload your documentation one file at a time.

    1. Log in to Accessible Learning Online.
    2. Under the Accommodations heading in the left-hand navigation, click Additional Documentation Submission.
    3. Select Start New Application.
    4. In the Basic Information Box, select the current term.
    5. In the List of Questions box, select Updated Documentation or New Documentation. Complete the required information.
    6. Click Submit Request.
    7. In the File Information box, enter the File Title for your documentation.
    8. Click Upload File.
