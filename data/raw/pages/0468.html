    Laurier Work Study Program

    Are you a full-time domestic student looking for a part-time job on campus? If so, you may be eligible for the Laurier Work Study Program (LWSP). See below for more information about the program including the types of positions included and the benefits of participating in the program. 

    For more information on timelines and applying to have your eligibility assessed, see LWSP Eligibility and Job Applications for more information. If you've accepted an LWSP position (congratulations!), see Onboarding and Program Requirements for more information.

    If you're an undergraduate international student, you can check out the International Student Work Experience Program (ISWEP). If you're interested in more information about other on-campus job opportunities, see Jobs on Campus.

    What is the LWSP? 

    The LWSP is an experiential learning program that supports part-time on-campus job opportunities for full-time domestic students in financial need. The program provides eligible students with an opportunity to work on-campus and gain valuable work experience. As a part of your role, you will engage in experiential learning programming facilitated by the Career Centre.

    This programming will guide you in setting goals, keeping track of your progress in the role, and reflecting on your competencies and accomplishments. Once you complete your work term and programming, the position will be added to your Laurier Experience Record.

    Types of Positions

    The LWSP includes a variety of part-time on-campus positions. Depending on the role, jobs may be in-person, remote, or hybrid. There are two types of positions available to apply to: one- or two-term positions and short-term opportunities.

    One- or Two-Term Positions

    Positions typically average 5-10 hours/week. You will develop a schedule with your LWSP supervisor that fits your academic schedule. Students may only hold one position during each work term. 

    The minimum hourly rate for positions will be $17.95/hour (starting fall 2026); however, some specialized positions may offer a higher rate of pay.

    Examples of positions:

    • Content Creator, 5 hours/week – support marketing goals, create and update content for social media platforms, and attend campus events/activities to capture photos/videos.
    • Program Assistant, 10 hours/week – provide administrative program support, track student progress in program, maintain and update resources and templates, and moderate workshops and provide technical support.
    • Communications Assistant, 8 hours/week – support engagement with prospective and current students and promote faculty events and achievements to the general public through website updates and e-mail communications.

    Short-Term Opportunities

    These positions are for short-term (four-week maximum) events/projects and up to a maximum of 20 hours total.

    All short-term positions will be paid $17.95/hour (starting fall 2026).

    Examples of positions:

    • Event Assistant – assist with on-campus event by greeting exhibitors, unloading booth materials, helping exhibitors to their table and answering questions.
    • Parking Attendant – help manage parking, welcome visitors, and ensure wayfinding goes smoothly at an on-campus event.
    • Project Assistant – support program evaluation project by conducting an environmental scan, researching best practices at other institutions, and compiling findings into a brief report with references. 
    • Digital Archive Assistant – scan paper documentation to PDF and digitally file following established procedures.
    • Office Inventory Support Assistant – review and re-organize inventory of print resources, identify and remove older versions of resources, and update inventory tracking.

    Benefits of Participating in the LWSP

    • Make connections between your workplace experience and your personal and professional development
    • Increase awareness of transferable skills and an understanding of the significance of each work experience
    • Articulate what has been learned on the job and how to market these skills
    • Develop language that can be used on resumés, further education applications and in interviews
    • Include your work experience on your Laurier Experience Record. Once experiences are included on your LER, you can publish it and share it to LinkedIn, use it in your resumé or job interviews, and for further education applications.
