    Manage Your Program

    Whether you are completing a course-based master's, major research paper/project (MRP), master’s thesis, or a doctoral dissertation, understanding the process and timelines will help you in your plans for completion.

    If you are taking graduate-level courses, you must be admitted into the Faculty of Graduate & Postdoctoral Studies.

    As a degree-seeking graduate student:

    • You are enrolled in a specific graduate degree program within a faculty or school.
    • You must maintain continuous registration (each term, three terms per year) in order to maintain your registration status at Laurier.

    Graduate Study Pathways

    Registration Status

    Registration status defines your pursuit of the graduate program (based upon your application for admission and the program in which you are enrolled), and impacts the tuition, financial support, and degree time limits.

    In order to qualify as full-time, graduate students must:

    • Be pursuing studies as a full-time occupation.
    • Identify themselves as full-time graduate students.
    • Be designated by the university as full-time graduate students.
    • Be geographically available and visit the campus regularly. Without forfeiting full-time status, graduate students, while still under supervision, may be absent from their university (e.g. visiting libraries, doing field work, attending a graduate course at another institution, etc.) provided that, if any such period of absence exceeds four weeks in any one term, written evidence shall be available in the Graduate Studies Office to the effect that the absence has the approval of the chairperson of the department and the dean of Graduate and Postdoctoral Studies.

    These are candidates who do not meet all of the requirements of a full-time master's or doctoral degree candidate.

    Normally, part-time students may not register in more than one full-credit course or equivalent per term.

    Circumstances that occasionally arise preventing you from remaining engaged in your academic work where you may need to request a leave, parental leave or withdraw from your program.

    Learn more about leave eligibility and the request process.

    You may be classified as a probationary student following a formal review by the department, school or faculty if:

    • you fail a course; or
    • you are permitted to continue in a program without fully meeting some requirements.

    You remain on probation until the course or its equivalent is successfully completed or until some predetermined requirement or condition has been met. Failure to remove your probationary status within three terms will result in you being required to withdraw from the program. For more information about academic probation, see the Graduate Academic Calendar.

    Tuition Payment Plan

    The tuition payment plan allows for additional financial flexibility in meeting tuition obligations each term. Eligible graduate students can apply to the plan in advance of the start of each term. Specific eligibility requirements and deadlines apply.

    Personal well-being, as well as academic and professional development, are key goals of graduate education. Learn more about how the university supports its graduate students in achieving success.

    Supports and Services

    There are a number of supports and services available to you in your journey as a graduate student: 

    • Accessible Learning is available for graduate students with short- and long-term disabilities. 
    • Urgent support and counselling is available on- and off-campus for students struggling with mental illness, in addition to a myriad of additional mental health resources for students.
    • Other organizations and services, including the Special Constable Service, Graduate Students' Association and other resource centres and student groups, are available to help as well.
    • The Office of Human Rights and Conflict Management provides three distinct avenues to find the help you may need: human rights, sexual violence support, conflict management and Ombuds support.
    • The associate deans in FGPS are available to assist graduate students facing issues that impact their progression and success in their program.

    Policies and Procedures

    You are expected to familiarize yourself with university policies. 

    • Academic, Research and Non-Academic Misconduct: Academic dishonesty, fraud, misconduct and failure to respect the rights of others are all offences under this code.
    • Research Ethics: Information on Laurier's policy on ethics review of research involving humans, including a link to the online research ethics board application system.
    • Intellectual Property: Students who submit work to the university that is eligible for copyright protection, and which is submitted as a requirement of an academic program, are entitled to sole copyright ownership.
