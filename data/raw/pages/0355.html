    Learning Strategies

    At Accessible Learning, our Learning Strategists work collaboratively with students with disabilities to develop personalized academic success plans. Whether you're navigating challenges with time management, attention, mental health, or other disability-related impacts, we're here to support you.

    Together, we'll identify strategies that align with your strengths and goals, building a practical toolkit that covers key areas such as time management, study skills, and organization. Our aim is to help you gain the confidence and skills to apply these strategies independently throughout your studies at Laurier.

    Meet Your Learning Strategy Team

    What Does a Learning Strategist Do?

    A learning strategist works with you to:

    • Understand how your disability may influence your academic experience.
    • Identify barriers that may affect your learning.
    • Develop effective study habits, time management routines, and focus strategies.
    • Build confidence, motivation and academic independence.

    Appointments are personalized to reflect your unique needs, goals, and learning preferences—ensuring the support your receive is meaningful and relevant to your academic journey at Laurier.

    Do I Need Learning Strategy Support?

    You will benefit from meeting with a Learning Strategist if you:

    • Struggle with time management or staying organized.
    • Feel overwhelmed by deadlines and academic expectations.
    • Experience challenges with focus, memory, or getting started on tasks.
    • Want to try new approaches to studying, reading, or writing.
    • Believe your disability may be impacting your learning or academic performance.

    Don't wait until things feel unmanageable—learning strategy support is most effective when accessed early and often.

    How to Prepare for a Learning Strategy Appointment

    To get the most from your Learning Strategy appointment:

    • Reflect on any challenges you are experiencing in your courses or studying.
    • Bring helpful materials like your course syllabus, assignment details or calendar (if you have one).
    • Come with an open mind—we will explore new tools and techniques together and guide you every step of the way.

    Remember - we are here to support you in building the strategies that work for you! No pressure—just a collaborative space to help you thrive at Laurier.

    Earn Your ASk Certificate

    Appointments with a Learning Strategist in Accessible Learning can count towards the Student Success Academic Skills (ASk) Certificate. This is great way of recognize your effort as you build your academic skills! Speak with your Learning Strategist if you want to learn more or if you are already working towards your ASk Certificate.

    Studying and Learning

    Do you feel distracted when you try to study or get work done? Does your mind wander or are you sensitive to background noise? These techniques help create an environment for deep focus.  

     Who This Helps  

    Students with disabilities who:  

    • Are easily distracted or experience mental fatigue . 
    • Find it hard to maintain attention on one task . 
    • Difficulty filtering out background noise or environmental distractions.

    • Need structured breaks to stay productive . 

    Strategies to Apply and Instructions

    • Pomodoro Technique – Work for 25 minutes, break for 5; repeat. Remember to use your phone only on your timed breaks.  
    • Focus-Friendly Environment – Use noise-canceling headphones, declutter workspace and silence your phone.  
    • Distraction Log – Write down intrusive thoughts and revisit later.  
    • Mindful Transitions – Reset focus between tasks with breathing or stretching.  
    • Single-Tasking – Approach one task at a time and while on the computer use a single-tab browser.  

     Recommended Apps 

    • Forest App - Forest helps you stay focused on the important things in life. Whenever you want to stay focused, plant a tree.  
    • Cold Turkey - Block websites, games and applications to boost your productivity and reclaim your free time.  
    • RescueTime - Improve your focus and automate your time tracking. Goal tracking, website and application blocking.  
    • StayFocused - Boost your productivity by limiting the amount of time you spend on time-wasting websites.  
    • MindBell - Rings a mindfulness bell as a reminder throughout the day to momentarily stop and consider what you are currently doing.  
    • ActivityWatch - Automatically monitors how you spend time on your devices—tracking productivity, time spent on various projects, and screen habits—to help you better understand and optimize your daily routines.
    • SelfControl - Free Mac application to help you avoid distracting websites. 

    • PawBlock - Browser extension that allows you to block distracting websites.

    Do you study for hours but forget everything during the test? These memory-based techniques help you store and retrieve information more effectively.  

    Who This Helps 

    Students with disabilities who:  

    • Struggle to remember what they’ve studied.  
    • Forget information quickly without repeated exposure.  
    • Trouble retrieving facts or concepts during tests.  
    • Looking to improve long-term retention of academic material.  

    Strategies to Apply and Instructions 

    • Retrieval Practice – Quiz yourself without looking at your notes.

    • Method of Loci – Use memory palaces to organize information spatially.

    Do you reread the same paragraphs without remembering what you read? Do you highlight everything, making it hard to review later? These strategies help you engage with reading and retain key information.  

     Who This Helps 

    Students with disabilities who:  

    • Struggle with focus and attention while reading.  
    • Have difficulty processing and organizing large amounts of text.  
    • Find it hard to remember or understand what they’ve just read.  
    • Need more structure to stay engaged during reading.  

     Strategies to Apply and Instructions

    • SQ3R Method – Survey, Question, Read, Recite, and Review.  
    • Concept or Mind Mapping – Create a visual web of connected ideas.  
    • Planning- Ensure you block time off on your calendar for each class weekly to complete your readings

    Do you spend too long trying to make things perfect? Avoid starting because you’re afraid it won’t be “good enough”? These strategies help you manage high standards and focus on progress, not perfection. 

    Who This Helps 

    Students with disabilities who: 

    • Struggle to start or finish assignments because of pressure to get it “just right”. 
    • Spend excessive time on small details. 
    • Experience anxiety or burnout from overworking. 
    • Have trouble submitting work unless it feels 100% complete. 
    • Need support in breaking down large tasks and building self-trust. 

     Strategies to Apply and Instructions 

    • The 80% Rule – Remember “Done is better than perfect.” Aim for 80% quality on the first attempt—you can revise later if needed. 
    • Time Limits for Tasks – Set time boundaries to avoid hyper-focusing. Use timers (Pomodoro = 25 min work, 5 min break) and stick to them. Treat the limit as a “soft deadline” to move forward. 
    • “Good Enough” Checklist – Create a short checklist of what must be included in the task. Once all items are checked, the work is considered ready—even if it doesn’t feel perfect. 
    • Break It Down, Then Start Small – Divide the assignment into micro-steps (e.g., open the doc, write the title, jot three bullet points). Starting with a small step reduces overwhelm and builds momentum. 
    • Self-Compassion Reframes – Catch perfectionist thoughts (“It has to be amazing”) and replace them with supportive ones (“This is a first step, not the final product”). 

    Recommended Apps 

    • Focus To-Do – Timer-based tool to prevent overworking and encourage regular breaks. 
    • Notion – Use for tracking small, manageable tasks within bigger projects. 
    • Mindful Affirmations – App for Apple users with gentle reminders and mindset prompts 
    • Google Docs Comments – Leave self-comments to flag ideas for future edits, rather than fixing everything now 
    • Forest – Stay focused for a set time, then “let go” once the timer ends

    Do you delay starting assignments, even when you know they’re important? Do you wait until the last minute, feel overwhelmed, or avoid tasks altogether? These strategies are designed to help you recognize avoidance patterns and build momentum with realistic action steps.    

    Who This Helps 

    Students with disabilities who:  

    • Struggle with executive functioning, particularly task initiation and self-regulation.  
    • Become overwhelmed by big or unstructured tasks.  
    • Wait for motivation to strike before starting work.  
    • Benefit from structured routines, small goals, and external accountability.  

    Strategies to Apply and Instructions 

    • The 5-Minute Rule – Commit to working for just 5 minutes. Often, starting is the hardest part.  
    • Implementation Intentions – Use “If–Then” planning: If it’s 4 PM, then I’ll open my reading notes.  
    • Time Blocking – Schedule short, focused work blocks with built-in breaks to reduce overwhelm.  
    • Temptation Bundling – Pair work with something enjoyable (e.g., studying at a café with a treat). 
    • Just Start Anywhere – Begin with any part of the task — it doesn’t have to be the beginning. 
    • Work With a Friend or Body Double – Use virtual or in-person co-working for accountability.

    • Forest – Grow a virtual tree while staying focused. 
    • Pomodoro Timer – Web-based Pomodoro tracker. 
    • Focus To-Do – Combines a Pomodoro timer with task lists.

    • Flow Club – Virtual co-working spaces to get started with others.  
    • MindBell – Use mindful bell intervals to return your focus gently.  
    • Google Calendar – Set recurring focus blocks and deadlines.

    Do you leave lectures with messy or unclear notes? Wondering how to turn them into something useful for studying? These strategies help you transform your class notes into active, effective study tools. 

    Who This Helps 

    Students with disabilities who: 

    • Struggle to recall lecture content after class. 
    • Need step-by-step routines to process and reinforce material. 
    • Learn better through repetition and organization. 
    • Benefit from breaking study into manageable parts. 

    Strategies to Apply and Instructions 

    The Study Cycle Framework –     A 5-step process: 

    • Preview: Skim the topic before class (e.g., syllabus, headings) 
    • Attend: Be present and take notes during lecture 
    • Review: Within 24 hours, clarify and clean up your notes 
    • Study: Use active strategies (see below) to quiz and test yourself 
    • Check: Self-assess your understanding regularly 

     Note Transformation – Rewrite or type notes using methods like: 

    • Cornell Notes (Divide your page into cues, notes, and summary) 
    • Mind Maps or Concept Maps 
    • Color-Coding or Highlighting Key Terms 

     Make It Active 

    • Convert notes into flashcards or practice questions 
    • Teach the content out loud to yourself or a peer 
    • Create summary sheets for each topic 

     Weekly Review – Schedule a 15–20-minute weekly review session per course to go over past material and reinforce memory using spaced repetition 

    Recommended Apps 

    • Notion – All-in-one workspace for organizing notes, flashcards, and summaries 
    • OneNote – A flexible digital notebook with tagging and visual tools 
    • Coggle – Easy-to-use mind mapping app for connecting concepts visually 
    • Quizlet – Turn your notes into flashcards, games, and quizzes 
    • Focus To-Do – Combines Pomodoro timing with task lists to stay on track 
    • Anki - Powerful spaced repetition tool for long-term retention

    Planning & Time Management

    Big assignments feel overwhelming? Not sure where to start? These strategies help you break large tasks into steps and reduce procrastination and anxiety. 

    Who This Helps

    Students with disabilities who:  

    • Struggle with task initiation and time estimation.  
    • Avoid or delay starting large projects. 
    • Get overwhelmed without a clear plan.  
    • Benefit from structured checklists and intermediate goals.  

     Strategies to Apply and Instructions

    • Chunk the Assignment – Break into 4–6 clear steps (e.g., research → outline → draft → edit).  
    • Use a Backward Timeline – Start with the due date and assign mini-deadlines.  
    • Checklist Everything – Turn each step into small, checkable actions.  
    • Use a Progress Tracker – Visualize your movement across the task.  
    • Write Just One Paragraph or Section First – Don’t wait to “feel ready”.  

     Recommended Apps

    • Trello – A visual project management tool using boards, lists, and cards for organizing tasks and tracking progress.  
    • Notion – An all-in-one workspace for notetaking, task management, and project organization.  
    • Google Tasks – A simple to-do list app integrated with Gmail and Google Calendar  
    • myHomework Planner – A digital student planner to track assignments, tests, and classes in one place

    Do you lose momentum when switching between tasks? Do you leave projects half-finished or find it hard to refocus? These strategies help reduce cognitive load and maintain mental flow throughout your day.  

    Who This Helps

    • Avoid Multitasking – Focus on one task at a time for better retention and performance.

    Do you have trouble keeping up with deadlines, planning your day, or managing how long tasks take? These tools and strategies help you manage your time more effectively.  

     Who This Helps 

    Students with disabilities who:  

    • Struggle with executive functioning skills such as planning, scheduling, and time estimation.  
    • Lose track of time or underestimate time needed to complete tasks. 
    • Have difficulty balancing academic, personal, and work responsibilities.  
    • Benefit from external structure, visual scheduling, and routine reminders.  

    Strategies to Apply and Instructions 

    • Plan Your Week on Sunday: Block time off on your schedule for classes, study time, meals, Sunday night planning for the week ahead, gym, prayer/meditation and breaks.  
    • Use Color-Coding: In Google Calendar, Notion, or iStudiez, using varying colours to represent your courses or various activities (e.g., red for due dates). Try using the same colour as each of your courses for labs and tutorials, but a lighter shade.  For example, if you marked a course in dark blue, use light blue for the lab or tutorial. 
    • Estimate Time for Tasks: Use app 30/30 or Focus Booster to track more accurately how much time is needed to complete tasks. Use this information to improve your schedule.  
    • Use the Pomodoro Technique: 25 minutes of work, 5-minute break—repeat 4 times, then take a longer break.   
    • Prioritize Your Tasks: Use the PDF Eisenhower Matrix or Remember the Milk web-based application for labelling important or urgent tasks.

    • Focus To-Do – Combines task lists with Pomodoro timing

    Do multiple-choice questions (MCQs) confuse you, even when you know the material? These tips help you approach MCQs strategically.  

    Who This Helps 

    Students with disabilities who:  

    • Overthink or rush through multiple-choice questions.  
    • Miss key words or fall for trick answers.  
    • Struggle to apply process-of-elimination techniques.  
    • Benefit from a strategic, step-by-step approach to testing.  

    Strategies to Apply and Instructions 

    • Read the Question First – Understand the stem before reading options.  
    • Predict the Answer – Think before checking the choices.  
    • Underline Keywords – Identify task words in the question.  
    • Use Elimination – Rule out obviously wrong choices.

    Does your mind go blank during exams even when you studied? Do feel very anxious before or during your exams? These techniques help you reduce anxiety and build test-day confidence.  

    Who This Helps 

    Students with disabilities who:  

    • Freeze up or blank out during exams despite preparation.  
    • Experience worry or panic leading up to or during a test.  
    • Need calming strategies to manage physical or emotional symptoms. 
    • Benefit from early preparation and familiarization with exam settings.  

    Strategies to Apply and Instructions 

    • Cognitive Restructuring – Replace “I’ll fail” with “I am prepared and will try my best”.  
    • Exam Rehearsal – Simulate the test setting and time limits. 
    • Grounding Techniques – 5-4-3-2-1 method or breathing exercises.  
    • Preparation Planning – Start studying weeks ahead to reduce stress.  
    • Progressive Muscle Relaxation – Practice tensing/releasing your muscles to calm your body.
