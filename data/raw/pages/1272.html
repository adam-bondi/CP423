    Barry Kay Lecture Series

    The Barry Kay Lecture Series was established in honour of Dr. Barry Kay, who was a professor in the Department of Political Science for 41 years at Laurier. When Kay passed away in December 2019, the department lost a cherished and dear colleague. 

    Join us for our annual lecture series to learn about the world of politics from experts in the field and engage in discussions.

    About Barry Kay

    Dr. Barry Kay was a respected scholar and media commentator on politics at both the federal and provincial levels in Canada and in the United States. After receiving a PhD from the University of Rochester, he went on to author or co-author more than 40 academic articles and book chapters. In addition to his scholarly work, Dr. Kay wrote hundreds of newspaper opinion articles and was frequently interviewed by journalists across Canada and the U.S. Among his many activities, he was an active member of the Laurier Institute for the Study of Public Opinion and Policy (LISPOP) and developed a widely consulted seat-projection model based on electoral data and polls.

    Read more about Dr. Kay's legacy in these pieces from Laurier's own Campus Magazine and one of Canada's national newspapers, the Globe and Mail.  

    The Lectures

    Friday, November 11, 2022 from 2:30-4:30 pm

    Watch the lecture.

    Join us for a panel discussion organized by Barry's colleagues in the Department of Political Science, featuring American and Canadian experts.  

    The outcome of the 2022 US midterm elections will have important implications - not only for how the US is governed for the next two years but also in terms of the positioning of potential contenders for the presidency in 2024.  This panel will discuss what happened and why, as well as what the Midterm outcomes might mean for the 2024 presidential elections.

    Panelists: 

    Samara Klar, Associate Professor at the University of Arizona School of Government and Public Policy; studies how individuals’ personal identities and social surroundings influence their political attitudes and behavior. Her award-winning book, Independent Politics, (co-authored with Yanna Krupnikov) was published by Cambridge University Press in 2016 and her research appears in top journals including Science, Nature Human Behaviour, American Political Science Review, American Journal of Political Science, Journal of Politics,  among others. In 2016, Prof. Klar started the website WomenAlsoKnowStuff to promote women in political science.
     
    Gerry Boychuk, Professor of Political Science at the University of Waterloo; has served as Chair of the Department of Political Science, Director of Global Governance, and Director of the Master's of Public Service program.  He specializes in American and Canadian politics and comparative historical political development.  His book, National Health Insurance in Canada and the United States: Race, Territory, and the Roots of Difference, was awarded the Canadian Political Science Association's Smiley Prize for the Best Book on Government and Politics in Canada.  He is currently writing a manuscript, Not a Tinker's Damn: Female Suffrage in the Canadian Prairie Provinces and Northern Plains States, 1912-1918.                 
     
                  
    Moderator:
    Debora L. VanNijnatten, Professor of Political Science at Wilfrid Laurier University; Professor of Political Science at Wilfrid Laurier University. Her research focuses on Canadian and American environmental and climate politics and policy, and she also studies how we jointly manage shared resources along the Canada-US border. She is the co-author of North American Environmental Policy: Capacity, Approaches and Transboundary Issue Management, co-editor of North American Climate Change Policy: Designing Integration in a Regional System and editor of Canadian Environmental Politics and Policy text (Oxford, 5th ed now in the works).

    Donations

    Barry wanted his legacy to reach his students by acknowledging their achievements, so three scholarships were established to recognize exceptional students in Political Science:  the Lillian and Harry Kay Memorial Scholarship, named for Barry's parents who instilled in him the value of education; the Sherry Palmer Scholarship, Political Science administrative assistant for 30 years, who Kay considered the “heart and soul” of the department; and the Barry Kay Scholarship established to honour Barry's love of teaching and high regard for his students. To contribute to one of these scholarships, please visit the memorial donation site.
